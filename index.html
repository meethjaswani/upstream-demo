How the shock math works

Everything below runs in your browser on the data in this page. Only the extraction of deals from documents was done ahead of time.

  1. Hop 1, the shock. A demand shock flows upstream, from buyer to seller. Seller's revenue lost = shock x annual value of what the buyer buys from it. Shown as % of the seller's revenue.
  2. Next hops. Each hit company cuts its own purchases in proportion to its % revenue hit, times a damping factor (default 0.6 per hop, adjustable). Clouds cut GPU orders, chip makers cut TSMC and SK Hynix orders.
  3. Loops. The graph has cycles, so we iterate up to 4 hops and stop early when no company moves by more than 0.1% of revenue.
  4. Equity hit (separate from revenue). Investor's stake value x valuation drop. Drop = 1.5 x shock (for the company in trouble) or x its % revenue hit (for others), capped at 100%. ESTIMATED, adjustable.
  5. Annual values. Multi-year contracts are spread evenly over their term. Undisclosed amounts use a stated placeholder (dotted arrows). Each link says how it was estimated.
  6. Leverage flag. Oracle (free cash flow -$23.7B, $43B debt raised in FY26) and CoreWeave (debt-funded buildout) carry a "Leverage amplifies this" chip: the same revenue gap hurts more when it services debt.
  7. Supply shock. Flows downstream instead: buyers lose the share of their business that depends on the seller's deliveries, with the same damping.

Not investment advice. A first-order model to show where exposure sits, not a price forecast.
