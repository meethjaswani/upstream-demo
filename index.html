How it works

  1. The graph. Companies are nodes. A link means "A sells to B", taken from filings, press releases or credible reports.
  2. Link strength. Each link gets a probability that a shock passes through it: p = base × size × confidence.
    Base is the disclosed revenue share if one exists. Otherwise it is 1 ÷ the number of buyers the seller has (the weighted cascade of Kempe, Kleinberg and Tardos, 2003).
    Size is √(buyer market cap ÷ seller market cap), capped at 1.
    Confidence is 1.0 for disclosed links, 0.7 for reported and 0.4 for estimated.
  3. The start. The AI reads your scenario and picks only the starting companies and a severity. It never picks who gets hit.
  4. The cascade. Each hit company gets one chance to pass the shock along each link, with probability p. This runs 20,000 times with random draws (Monte Carlo). Each company is hit at most once per run, so loops cannot blow up.
  5. The output. P(hit) is the share of runs in which a company was hit. Predicted drop is severity, times 0.6 for each extra hop, averaged over runs. Market cap at risk is predicted drop × market cap.

Known limits. Links without a disclosed revenue share count equally. Only signed deals are seen, not expectations. Shocks are treated as independent. The 0.6 damping and the confidence weights are assumptions, applied the same way everywhere.
